📊 Demographics
Racial composition of the Pullman neighborhood over time
Neighborhood history
1880s
Pullman opens as a planned company town in 1880. Many of its first residents are European immigrants, and the town is almost entirely white.
1910s–40s
The Great Migration brings hundreds of thousands of Black Southerners to Chicago, but housing discrimination, including racially restrictive covenants, confines most Black families to the Black Belt farther north. Pullman and neighboring Roseland remain overwhelmingly white.
1955–60
After World War II, suburban growth, backed by federal mortgage programs that largely excluded Black buyers, begins drawing white families out of city neighborhoods. The Far South Side's industrial economy starts to change.
1960s
In 1960 a redevelopment proposal threatens the original town with total demolition for an industrial park. Residents form the Pullman Civic Organization and lobby the city to save it. Across the Far South Side, blockbusting and white flight drive rapid racial change; in neighboring Roseland, most white families have left by the early 1970s.
1970–72
Led by Americo Lisciotto, the Pullman Civic Organization's campaign succeeds: the Pullman Historic District is listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1969, becomes a National Historic Landmark in 1970, and is designated a Chicago Landmark in 1972.
1973–80
In 1975 the Historic Pullman Foundation buys the Hotel Florence to save it from demolition. In 1980 Wisconsin Steel in nearby South Deering closes abruptly, costing thousands of jobs, and other Calumet mills follow. Unemployment rises across the Far South Side.
1980s–90s
Disinvestment deepens as mills close, businesses leave, and population falls. In 1981 the Pullman car plant closes for good. In 1991 the State of Illinois takes title to the Hotel Florence, now part of a state historic site.
2000s
Residents, preservationists, and community groups keep restoring homes and press for federal recognition, building the case that leads to national monument status.
2015–now
On February 19, 2015, President Obama designates Pullman a National Monument. Method Products opens a soap factory on a former steel mill site on 111th Street, the neighborhood's first new manufacturing plant in about 30 years. The National Park Service visitor center opens in the restored Administration Building in 2021, and in December 2022 Pullman becomes a National Historical Park.
Sources: U.S. Census Bureau decennial census; CMAP Community Data Snapshots; Big Shoulders Atlas (2011). Figures reflect the Pullman community area (tracts 5001–5003). The national park boundary covers a subset of this area.
